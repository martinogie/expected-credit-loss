How it works

  • Probability of default (PD): four multinomial logistic models, one per delinquency state (current, 30, 60, 90 days), predict next month's state from grade, months on book, unemployment and recent delinquency. Chaining them month by month gives each loan's chance of charge-off in every future month, under any unemployment path.
  • Loss given default (LGD): 1 − present value of recoveries ÷ balance at charge-off, by grade. Only charge-offs with a full 12-month recovery window are used (LGD ).
  • Exposure at default (EAD): the scheduled balance three months before charge-off, discounted at each loan's own rate.
  • Scenarios: four 24-month unemployment paths, reverting to a 4.6% long-run rate over the following 12 months, weighted 15/45/30/10. The September paths are worse than June's, which drives the macro line in the roll-forward.

All data is synthetic: 70,000 loans from 2021–2026, simulated with known grade, seasoning, macro and re-default effects so the models can be checked against the truth. Built with AI-assisted coding (Claude); the model design, validation and review are my own.
